Introduction for Administrators

This guide is for the person who sets up and runs ShuleNova for a school: the system administrator. It covers what only an administrator does: the school's structure (years, terms, classes), user accounts and what each role may open, the settings that change how the system behaves, and the licence. Day-to-day work (marking a register, recording a payment, printing a report form) is in the User Guide, which every user can open.

Work on the live site with care. Everything you change here affects every user at once, and every change is recorded in the audit trail under your name. Where a page asks you to confirm, read the message before you click.

What the Administrator Does

  • Sets up the school: its details and logo, the academic years and terms, the classes and streams, the calendar of events, and the lists that fill the drop-down boxes all over the system.
  • Manages people's access: creates a user account for each member of staff (and, if the school wants, for parents and learners), gives each account its roles, resets forgotten passwords and removes accounts that are no longer needed.
  • Decides what each role sees: which menus and pages each role can open, and who approves expenses, budgets, schemes of work and other requests.
  • Keeps an eye on the system: who is signed in, what has been changed and by whom, and any errors the system has recorded.
  • Looks after the licence: checks when it expires and installs a new key from Kodetek when the school renews or adds a module.

Administrator, SuperAdministrator and Other Roles

Every user account holds one or more roles. A role is a name (Teacher, Accounts, HeadTeacher…) with a set of menus attached to it. A user with several roles sees everything each of their roles sees.

RoleWhat it means
AdministratorManages users and roles, menu permissions, approval workflows, the logs and the licence, and opens the administrator guide. What an Administrator sees in the side menu still follows the menus ticked for the Administrator role in Security > Menu Permissions.
SuperAdministratorSees every menu, whatever Menu Permissions say. Only a SuperAdministrator can give the SuperAdministrator role to someone or take it away, set up the SMS gateway, and reverse a request that has already been approved. Usually held by Kodetek's support account and at most one senior person at the school. Give it together with Administrator.
ViewerView-only. Given on top of another role, the user sees what that role sees and can change nothing. For board members, auditors or a demonstration login. See The Viewer Role.
Other rolesTeacher, HeadTeacher, DeputyHeadTeacher, HeadOfDepartment, Accounts and the rest. Each sees the menus ticked for it in Menu Permissions. You can add roles of your own.
Keep the Administrator role to a few people. An Administrator can create accounts and change anyone's access. Give it to the one or two people who run the system, not to every head of section.

Setting Up a New School: the Order to Follow

Much of ShuleNova depends on things set up before it: a class needs a year and a learning level, a learner needs a class, a mark needs a subject. Setting things up in this order saves going back.

Then, for step 11:

  • Finance: the chart of accounts, fee and expense categories, fee structures and the finance settings. See the Finance chapter.
  • Payroll: earning and deduction types, tax and NSSF bands, reliefs, and each employee's salary. See the Payroll chapter.
  • Elections: the elections settings, then each election's posts and polling stations. See the Elections chapter.
  • Communications: the SMS gateway (by Kodetek or a SuperAdministrator), message templates and where parents' numbers come from. See the Communications chapter.
Each new year. At the start of every year you repeat steps 3 to 5 (a new year, its terms and its classes; the Classes page can copy last year's classes for you), then move learners up with Students > Class Assignment.

The Licence

ShuleNova is sold as a core system plus paid modules. The core (dashboard, school, students, staff, settings, reports and security) is always included. The paid modules are:

  • Academics (with Teaching Management): assessments, exams, report forms, schemes of work, lesson plans and records of work.
  • Finance: budgets, fees, invoices, payments, sponsors, expenses and accounts.
  • Payroll: salaries, loans, payroll processing and payslips.
  • Elections: student elections, polling stations and results.
  • Communications: SMS and email messages to parents and staff.

The licence key Kodetek installs for the school says which modules it includes and until when. A module that is not licensed simply does not appear: its menus, its dashboard cards, its sections in Settings and its chapters in these guides are all hidden. Its data is not deleted. The Licence explains how to read the licence and install a new key.

How These Guides Work

The two book icons at the top right of every page open the guides:

  • The User Guide is for everyone who signs in.
  • The Administrator Guide (this one) shows only to users holding the Administrator or SuperAdministrator role.
  • Both show only the chapters and sections for the modules your licence includes, so they always match what the school can use.

To hide both guides from everyone (for example on a demonstration site), switch off Settings > Global Settings > Security Settings > Show user guides and save. The icons are gone for each user the next time they sign in or refresh the page.

School Set-up

This chapter covers the school's structure: its details, the academic years and terms, the classes, the calendar, and the lists behind the drop-down boxes. Set these up first, in the order of the set-up checklist; almost every other page depends on them.

Most pages here work the same way: a list, an Add… button at the top right that opens a form, and an edit (pencil) and delete (bin) icon on each row. Where a list has filters (year, curriculum), choose them and click Search.

Deleting. Do not delete a year, term, class or list item that is already in use. Records that point to it (learners, marks, invoices) depend on it. Correct its name instead, or close it.

School Details

School > Details holds the school's name, logo and contacts, and the headings printed on report forms and other documents. The page opens read-only.

Editing the school details

  1. Click Edit school details at the top right. The fields open for editing and a Select logo box appears under the logo.
  2. Fill in or correct the fields (see the table below). To change the logo, click Choose File and pick an image; a square logo looks best.
  3. Under School levels available, tick the levels the school runs: Pre-Primary, Lower Primary, Upper Primary, Junior school, Senior school.
  4. Click Update Records and confirm. Click Stop editing school details to leave without saving.
FieldUsed for
School name, email, phone number, address, websitePrinted on report forms, receipts, statements and letters.
InitialsThe school's short name, used in codes and short headings.
Report header, title, subtitle, title detailsThe heading lines at the top of printed reports.
Motto, vision, missionThe school's statements, kept with its details.
Only Administrators. The Edit school details link shows only to users holding the Administrator role.

Academic Years

School > Academic Years lists the school years. Every term, class and set of marks belongs to a year, so create the year before anything else in it.

Adding a year

  1. Click Add academic year.
  2. Type the Academic year (for example 2027) and its Abbreviation.
  3. Set the Start date and End date, and a Rank: the order the years are listed in (1, 2, 3…).
  4. Switch Is the year active? on for the current year. The list shows an active year as Open and the others as Closed.
  5. Click Save.
One open year. Keep only the current year open. Pages that pick "this year" by default (elections, for example) take the open year.

Sessions (Terms)

School > Sessions holds the terms of each year. Choose the year and curriculum at the top and click Search to see that year's terms.

Adding a term

  1. Click Add session.
  2. Choose the Curriculum and the Academic year.
  3. Type the Session name (Term 1) and an Abbreviation, and choose the Session type (normally Term).
  4. Give it a Rank (1 for Term 1, 2 for Term 2…).
  5. Set the Start date (opening day) and End date (closing day). The end date cannot be before the start date.
  6. Switch Is the session active? on for the current term, and click Save.
Get the dates right. ShuleNova works out "the current term" from the dates: the term that today falls in, or during a holiday the last term that has started. The dashboard and the pages that open on the current term use it. The dates also print on report forms ("This term ends on / Next term begins on").

Classes

A class in ShuleNova is a learning level (a grade, such as Grade 4) plus a stream (East, West…) in one academic year: Grade 4E 2026. Learners are placed in a class each year, and teachers are allocated to it. The learning levels and streams themselves are set up in Dropdowns.

School > Classes lists a year's classes. Choose the year (and if you like the curriculum and level) and click Search. Each row shows the class leaders and, in Students, how many learners are in it; click the number to see them.

Adding a class

  1. Click Add school class.
  2. Choose the Academic year, the Class/Learning level and the Stream.
  3. Type the Class name as it should appear on lists and reports (for example Grade 4E 2026) and a Rank (the order classes are listed in: Day Care first, Grade 9 last).
  4. Click Save.

Copying last year's classes into a new year

At the start of a year you do not need to add every class by hand.

  1. Create the new academic year first.
  2. On the Classes page choose the new year at the top.
  3. Click Copy classes from another year, pick the year to copy from, and click Copy classes.

Each class is copied with its level, stream and rank, and the year in its name is changed. Learners and class leaders are not copied (move learners up with Students > Class Assignment). Classes the new year already has are left as they are, so it is safe to run twice.

Class leaders

Click the spanner in the Leadership column to open a class and add its leaders: a learner, teacher or parent in a leadership role (class monitor, class president, Class CEO…). The roles themselves are the Leadership Roles list in Dropdowns.

Events

School > Events is the school calendar: opening and closing days, exams, sports day, trips. The next few events show on everyone's dashboard. Choose the year and click Search to see its events.

Adding an event

  1. Click Add event.
  2. Choose the Academic year and the term (Session name).
  3. Type the Event name and Event location, set the start and end dates, add a description if useful, and click Save.

How many upcoming events the dashboard shows is set in Global Settings (Upcoming Events on Dashboard).

Dropdowns

Settings > Dropdowns holds the lists that fill the drop-down boxes across the system. The sections are down the left; click a section to open it and a list to see its items on the right.

Adding or changing an item

  1. Open the section and click the list (for example Learning Levels).
  2. Click Add… at the top of the list, or the pencil on a row to change it.
  3. Fill in the fields. Most lists have a Name, a Rank (the order items are listed in boxes) and a Description.
  4. Click Save (or Update).

The sections are:

  • System Settings: the general lists, below. Always there.
  • Academics: Curricula and Subject Groups, and links to Subjects, Edu-Level Subjects and the Grading System. Exam Types are here too; this section is covered in the Academics Set-up chapter.
  • Finance: the Chart of Accounts, Fee Categories and Expense Categories. See the Finance chapter.
  • Payroll Settings: links to Earning Types, Deduction Types, Tax Bands, NSSF Bands, Reliefs and Rates & Limits. See the Payroll chapter.
  • CBE Settings: assessment types, competencies, values, co-curricular and community service lists, and the curriculum design pages. See the Academics Set-up chapter.

Users and Security

Everyone who uses ShuleNova signs in with their own account. This chapter covers creating those accounts, giving them roles, choosing what each role can open, and checking who has done what. The pages are under the Security menu.

Can't see the Security menu? A SuperAdministrator always sees it. Anyone else sees it only if Security pages are ticked for one of their roles in Menu Permissions. An Administrator can also open the pages directly by their address, for example /security/users.

Users

Security > Users lists every account: the username, email and name, the person it is linked to (a member of staff, a learner or a parent) and its roles. Type in Search to find an account by username, email, name or linked person.

Each row has three buttons: edit (pencil), Reset Password (key) and delete (bin).

Creating an account

Add the person first (Staff > Staff Details, or the learner or parent), then:

  1. Click Add User.
  2. Under Linked Person, click Staff, Student or Parent, then type a name, UPI or email in Person and pick the person. ShuleNova fills in the first name, last name and email from their record; you can still change them.
  3. Type a Username (short and easy to type, such as the first name) and check the Email. Both must be unique.
  4. Type a starting Password of at least 6 characters. The eye button shows what you typed.
  5. Under Roles, tick the roles the person needs (for a teacher, Teacher; for the bursar, Accounts; and so on).
  6. Click Save and confirm.

Give the person their username and starting password. At their first sign-in ShuleNova makes them choose their own password before they continue.

Link staff accounts. Linking is optional, but many pages depend on it. A teacher sees "my classes", "my subjects" and their own schemes of work only through the staff record their account is linked to, and a head of department sees their department the same way. A person can be linked to one account only.

Changing an account or its roles

  1. Click the pencil on the account.
  2. Change the details, tick or untick roles, or link a different person (Clear removes the link).
  3. Leave Password blank to keep the current one.
  4. Click Update. The role changes are saved straight after the details.

A user's new roles take effect the next time they sign in.

The lock on SuperAdministrator. If you are not a SuperAdministrator yourself, the SuperAdministrator box is greyed out with a padlock: only a SuperAdministrator can give that role or take it away.

Resetting a forgotten password

ShuleNova does not send password reset emails. When someone forgets their password:

  1. Click the key button on their account.
  2. Type a temporary password (at least 6 characters) and click Reset Password.
  3. Give the temporary password to the person privately. At their next sign-in they must choose a new one.

Removing an account

When someone leaves, click the bin on their account and confirm. The account is deleted and can no longer sign in; the audit trail still shows what was done under their name.

Roles

Security > Roles lists the roles, with how many users hold each one.

  • Click the eye button on a role to see who holds it: their name, username and email.
  • Click Add Role to make a new role (for example Librarian), then choose its menus in Menu Permissions and give it to users on the Users page.
  • The pencil renames a role; the bin deletes it.
Do not rename or delete the built-in roles. ShuleNova looks for some roles by name: Administrator, SuperAdministrator, Teacher, HeadTeacher, HeadOfDepartment, Accounts and Viewer among them. Renaming or deleting one of these breaks the features that rely on it. Add new roles instead.

The Viewer role

Viewer makes an account view-only. Give it on top of another role: the user sees exactly what that other role sees, and can change nothing. Administrator + Viewer sees the whole school; Accounts + Viewer sees the finance pages. It suits:

  • a board member or the school's owner who wants to look but not touch;
  • an auditor checking the books;
  • a shared demonstration login.

A viewer's pages look normal, buttons included, but every save, change or delete is refused with "This account is view-only. Changes are not saved.", and a yellow View only badge shows in their top bar. They can still sign in and out and print. A viewer cannot change their own password either, and is never asked to, so a shared login keeps working. In the Users form the Viewer box is marked view only.

Menu Permissions

Security > Menu Permissions decides which menus and pages each role can open. It is the main way to control access: a user sees in the side menu only the pages ticked for at least one of their roles, and the system refuses the rest even if they type the address.

  1. Click a role in Select Role on the left. Its menus appear on the right, grouped as in the side menu (Dashboard, School, Staff, Students…), with a count such as 5/6 of what is ticked in each group.
  2. Tick or untick pages. The box on a group's dark bar ticks or unticks the whole group; All and None do the whole list.
  3. Click Save. ShuleNova says how many pages will be added and removed; confirm.

Users see the change the next time they sign in or refresh the page.

What else is on the list

  • Dashboard cards: under Dashboard, each dashboard card (class exam performance, fees, CBE assessments, teaching documents, payroll, elections, messages) is a permission of its own, so the fees card can go to the bursar and not to teachers.
  • Permission flags: some entries are not pages but extra rights. Student Assessments (Admin) lets a role enter assessment marks for any teacher, class and subject, not only the role holder's own allocations. Whole-school Access (Admin) under Teaching Management lets a role see and open every teacher's schemes of work, lesson plans and records of work (heads of department see their own departments without it). Tick them only for the roles that need them.
  • Settings and set-up pages: saving on a set-up page (school details, years, terms, dropdowns, global settings) is accepted only from a role that holds that page.
  • Only the modules your licence includes are listed.
SuperAdministrator. Super Administrators have full access whatever is ticked for them here. Every other role, Administrator included, follows its ticks, so make sure the Administrator role holds every page administrators need.
Start small. A role with nothing ticked sees only the dashboard. When adding a role, tick what the job needs, sign in as a test user with that role, and add more if something is missing.

Active Users

Security > Active Users lists who is signed in now, with when they signed in and from which IP address. Click Refresh to update it. Use it, for example, before an update to see who is working.

The list comes from the audit trail: a user whose last sign-in within the last 10 hours was not followed by a sign-out. Someone who closed their browser without signing out stays listed until their session would have expired.

Audit Logs

Security > Audit Logs (the audit trail) records who did what and when: every record created, changed or deleted, every sign-in, failed sign-in and sign-out, and printed documents.

  1. Set the From and To dates (the page opens on today).
  2. Narrow it if you like: a User, an Action (Create, Update, Delete, Login, LoginFailed, Logout…), an Entity (the kind of record), or words in the search box.
  3. Click Search. Export CSV saves every matching row to open in Excel.

Click the eye on a row to see the detail: who, when, from where, and for a change the Old values and New values side by side.

Several failed sign-ins? Filter on the action LoginFailed. Many failures for one username can mean someone is guessing a password; reset it and tell the user.

Error Logs

Security > Error Logs lists errors the system has recorded, for Kodetek's support team and for you. Filter by date, Level (Error, Warn), Status (Open only, Resolved only, All) and search, then click Search.

  • View opens the full entry: the message, the page address and the technical detail.
  • A badge such as ×3 means the same message appears several times on the page, most likely one incident.
  • Once a problem is dealt with, open it, add a note on what was done if you like, and click Mark resolved. Reopen undoes it.

When reporting a problem to Kodetek, give the time it happened and the page you were on; support can match it to the entry here.

System Settings

Three pages under Settings change how ShuleNova behaves for the whole school: Global Settings (choices such as how averages are worked out and when idle users are signed out), Approval Workflows (who approves what) and Licence.

Global Settings

Settings > Global Settings groups the settings into sections. Click a section's bar to open it (one opens at a time). Each setting has a short description beside it.

Changing settings

  1. Open a section and change the values you need. A changed setting is highlighted, its section shows "n changed", and the bar at the top counts the unsaved changes.
  2. You can change settings in several sections before saving.
  3. Click Save changes at the top and confirm. Only the settings you changed are saved.

If you leave the page without saving, the changes are lost. The bar says All settings are saved when nothing is waiting.

The sections

Only the sections for the modules your licence includes are shown.

Approval Edit Lock Policy

Under General Dashboard Settings, this decides when a record sent for approval (an expense, a budget, a scheme of work…) stops being editable:

  • Strict: locked as soon as it is submitted.
  • Lenient: still editable until the first approval step is approved.

An approved record is always locked.

Security settings

SettingWhat it does
Idle timeout (minutes)A user who does nothing for this long is signed out. Default 30. Use a shorter time where computers are shared, such as the staff room or the bursar's office.
Logout warning countdown (seconds)Before signing someone out, ShuleNova shows an "Idle mode detected" warning that counts down for this many seconds; they can click Resume to carry on. Default 60.

Approval Workflows

Some records need approving before they count: an expense before it is paid, a scheme of work before it is used. An approval workflow sets the steps a kind of record goes through and the role that approves each step. Settings > Approval Workflows has the form on the left and the workflows already set up on the right.

How approval works

  1. The workflow says, for each step, which role approves it (for example step 1 Reviewer: Accounts, step 2 Approver: HeadTeacher).
  2. When someone submits a record, they choose a person for each step from the users holding that step's role. If only one user holds the role, that person is picked for them.
  3. Each approver finds the request under the bell at the top of the page, and approves it, rejects it or returns it with a comment. When the last step (marked Final) approves, the record is approved and locked.

So for approval to work, the step's role must be given to the right people on the Users page.

The forms that can have a workflow

FormWhat is approved
Budget, Budget AmendmentA budget plan, and later changes to it.
Expense RequestAn expense, before it is paid.
Journal Entry, Credit / Debit NoteManual journal entries, and credit and debit notes.
PayrollA processed payroll, before it is locked and posted.
Scheme of WorkA teacher's scheme of work for a subject and class, once a term.
Lesson Plans (weekly)A week's lesson plans for one subject and class.

A form can have only one Active workflow at a time.

Changing a workflow

  1. Click the pencil on the workflow in the list. Its details open in the form on the left (Edit Workflow).
  2. Change the Name or Description if needed.
  3. For each step, set the Step Name (Reviewer, Approver, Head of Department…) and the Role that approves it. Use Add Step for another step, the arrows to change the order and the bin to remove one. The last step is always the Final one.
  4. Tick who is told at each step: Notify next (the next approver), Notify previous (the approver before) and Notify applicant (the person who submitted it).
  5. Leave Maker-checker (prevent self approval) ticked so that nobody can name themselves as an approver of their own request.
  6. Click Update. Cancel Edit leaves without saving.

To create a workflow for a form that has none, fill in the same form without clicking a pencil first, choose the Form, and click Create.

Changes apply to new requests. Requests already submitted keep the approvers chosen when they were sent. A request that has been approved can be reversed only by a SuperAdministrator.

Academics Set-up

Before teachers can enter marks, plan lessons or print report forms, the school's academic structure has to be in place: the subjects and which grades take them, the grading bands, the exam types, the CBE curriculum designs, and who teaches what. This chapter is for the person who sets all that up, usually the deputy head (academics) or the director of studies. Most of it is done once and then checked at the start of each year or term.

Two kinds of "grade". In ShuleNova a grade (Grade 4, PP 1) is a learning level, a year of schooling. The grading system is the score bands (EE, ME, AE, BE) used to grade marks. This chapter uses "grade" for the first and "grading" for the second.

The Grading System

Open Settings > Dropdowns > Academics > Grading System. Each row is one grade band: a mark range and the grade, points and remarks it gives. The demonstration school has both the CBE scales:

  • 4-Point: EE, ME, AE, BE, worth 4 to 1 points.
  • 8-Point: EE 1, EE 2, ME 1, ME 2, AE 1, AE 2, BE 1, BE 2, worth 8 to 1 points.

To add or change a band, click Add grade (or a row's edit button) and fill in the Curriculum, Category (4-Point or 8-Point), Grade name, Abbreviation, Minimum score and Maximum score, Points, Rank and the remarks in Swahili and English. The remarks print on report forms.

No gaps, no overlaps. The bands of one category must cover every mark from 0 to 100 exactly once (for example 0–24, 25–49, 50–74, 75–100). A mark that falls in no band gets no grade.

Which scale is used for exams, assessments, values and co-curricular scores is chosen in Global Settings.

Who Sees What: Academic Permissions

Finance Set-up

Before the bursar can raise an invoice or record an expense, ShuleNova needs the school's books set up: the chart of accounts, the fee and expense categories that point into it, the finance settings that say which accounts the automatic entries use, and the approval workflows. This chapter is for the administrator who does that, usually with the bursar beside them. Most of it is done once, when the school starts on ShuleNova; after that, the main yearly job is closing the accounting periods.

Ask the accountant. The chart of accounts is the school's accounting structure. Agree the account codes and names with the school's accountant or auditor before you enter them; changing them after a term's transactions have been posted is much harder.

Fee Categories

A fee category is a kind of fee: Tuition, Transport, Meals, Boarding, Activity, Uniform, Exam. Fee structures and invoices are made of them. Open Settings > Dropdowns > Finance > Fee Categories (also at /finance/fee-categories).

  • Name: as parents will see it on invoices.
  • Income Account: the income account its fees are posted to. Several categories may share one (an exam fee posted to tuition income, for example).
  • Rank: the order in which a payment pays the fees off when the school uses automatic apportioning. Rank 1 is paid first. Put the fees the school most needs collected first at the top.
  • Active: untick a category no longer charged.
Set the income account. The income journal of an invoice leaves out any fee whose category has no income account, so that income never reaches the books. Check every category has one before the first invoices are raised.

Expense Categories

An expense category is what the bursar chooses on each line of an expense: Electricity, Stationery, Repairs, Food Supplies and so on. Each points to the expense account it is posted to. Open Settings > Dropdowns > Finance > Expense Categories (also at /finance/expense-categories) and give each a Name, an Expense Account, a Rank (the order they are listed in) and a description.

Keep the list short and clear: an expense category that matches an account one to one makes the Expenses Report and the Profit & Loss agree line for line.

Approval Workflows for Finance

Expenses, journal entries, credit and debit notes and budget amendments are posted only when the last approver approves them. Each of these forms needs an active workflow under Settings > Approval Workflows (see Approval Workflows): without one, the form shows "No active workflow configured" and nothing can be approved. A common choice is two steps, the bursar or accountant as Reviewer and the head teacher as Approver, with maker-checker on so that nobody approves their own expense.

Approved records are locked. Only a SuperAdministrator can reverse an approval, which puts the record back to draft.

Closing and Reopening Periods

The bursar closes each period under Finance > Expenses & Accounts > Accounting Periods once it has ended and everything in it is approved (see the User Guide). Closing moves the surplus to Retained Earnings and locks every date in the period: no payment, invoice, expense or journal dated in it can be added, changed or deleted.

When a mistake in a closed period must be corrected, an Administrator or SuperAdministrator can click Reopen. Only the latest closed period can be reopened; its closing entry is taken out, the correction can be made, and the period is then closed again. Reopen only with the accountant's agreement: reports already given to the board for that period will change.

Close in order. A period cannot close while the one before it is still open, and a closed period can only be reopened after every later one has been reopened.

Payroll Set-up

Before the first payroll is run, ShuleNova needs the school's pay items and the statutory rules: what staff can earn, what can be deducted, the PAYE tax bands, the NSSF tiers, the tax reliefs, and the rates for SHIF and the Housing Levy. This is done once, and then changed only when the law or the school's policy changes. The monthly payroll itself is described in the user guide.

Where it lives. The statutory set-up is under Settings > Dropdowns > Payroll Settings, because it changes by law. The school's own choices (the employer KRA PIN, net pay rounding, the payroll accounts) are under Settings > Global Settings.

Recommended Set-up Order

  1. Global Settings > Payroll Settings: the school's KRA PIN and how net pay is rounded.
  2. Global Settings > Finance Settings and Staff Categories: the accounts the payroll journal uses (only if the school keeps its accounts in ShuleNova).
  3. Earning Types and Deduction Types: the pay items.
  4. Tax Bands, NSSF Bands and Reliefs: the PAYE and NSSF rules.
  5. Rates & Limits: SHIF, the Housing Levy and the retirement relief limits.
  6. Settings > Approval Workflows: who reviews and approves the payroll.
  7. Security > Menu Permissions: give the payroll officers' role the Payroll pages.
  8. Staff records: each employee's KRA PIN, ID, NSSF and SHA numbers and bank details; then their salaries under Payroll > Employee Salaries.

The payroll accounts

When a payroll is approved, ShuleNova builds its salary journal. The Finance Settings section of Global Settings says what happens and which accounts it uses:

  • Payroll Journal: Draft for review (recommended) leaves a filled-in journal under Journal Entries for finance to check and approve; Post automatically on approval puts it straight into the ledger; Off creates nothing.
  • PAYE Payable, NSSF Payable, SHIF Payable and Housing Levy accounts; Payroll Deductions Payable for other deductions without an account of their own; Staff Loans & Advances for loan recoveries.
  • Salaries are charged to the expense account on each staff category (Settings > Dropdowns > Staff Categories), so teaching and non-teaching pay stay apart. Default Salary Expense Account is used only for a category with no account; leave it blank to have approval stop and say so instead.

If the accounts are not set up at all, payroll can still be approved; no journal is made.

Earning Types

Earning types are the things staff can be paid. Basic Salary, House Allowance and Transport Allowance are marked System: they come from the columns of the salary grid and cannot be applied from here. Add the others your school pays, such as overtime, leave, hardship, acting or responsibility allowances.

  1. Click Add earning type.
  2. Type the Name and a short Code. Tick Taxable unless the law exempts it: a non-taxable earning is still paid but left out of taxable pay.
  3. Under Calculation, choose the Method: Fixed amount or Percentage of basic salary, and the amount or percentage.
  4. Switch on Applies to all staff only for an earning everyone gets. It is then paid to everyone on the payroll without adding it to each employee; someone with their own line for it keeps their own amount. Otherwise it is added per employee on the salary.
  5. Click Save.

Untick Active to retire an earning type rather than deleting one that has been used.

Tax deductible and Monthly capFor another deduction the law lets come off income before PAYE (a post-retirement medical fund, for instance), up to the cap. Leave the cap blank for none.
Liability accountWhere the money deducted is owed to (the pension scheme, the SACCO, the union). Leave on the general Payroll Deductions Payable account if you do not need to see each separately.
Insurance relief. A deduction that earns a tax relief, such as an insurance premium, is linked from the relief itself (see Reliefs), not here.

Tax Bands

The PAYE bands, applied in turn from the lowest: tax on the pay between each band's Lower Limit and Upper Limit at its Rate (%).

  1. Click Add tax band, type a Description (for example "Up to KES 24,000"), the Lower Limit, Upper Limit and Rate (%), and an Effective Date.
  2. Make each band's lower limit the previous band's upper limit, starting from 0. Give the top band a very large upper limit (for example 99,999,999).
Only active bands count. Every active band is used, whatever its effective date. When the law changes the bands, change the figures, or add the new bands and untick Active on the old ones, so that only one set is active.

NSSF Bands

NSSF is charged in tiers: each tier charges its rate on the pay between its two limits. The tiers sharing an effective date form one set, and each payroll month uses the most recent set in force for that month. The page shows each set with its dates, its maximum contribution a month and whether it is In use now, Not yet in force or Superseded.

  1. Click Add band. Type the Name (for example "NSSF Tier I"), the Tier number, the date it is Effective from, the Lower limit, Upper limit and Rate (%), and save.
  2. Add the next tier to the same set with the + Tier button on the set's header. Tier II's lower limit is normally Tier I's upper limit.
When the limits change. NSSF limits change most Februaries. Do not edit the old figures: add a new set dated from the day the new limits take effect. Re-processing an older month then still uses that month's limits. Payroll cannot be processed for a month with no NSSF set in force.

Reliefs

A relief comes off the tax itself, after the bands have been applied, such as personal relief and insurance relief. (A tax deductible deduction is different: it comes off income before the bands, and is set on the deduction type.)

  1. Click Add relief and type the Name and Code.
  2. Choose the Basis:
    • Fixed amount: a flat sum each month, such as personal relief. Switch on Applies to all staff to give it to everyone, or link it to a deduction under Worked out from to give it only to staff who pay that deduction.
    • Percentage of a deduction: a percentage of what the employee paid towards the deduction chosen under Worked out from, such as insurance relief on insurance premiums.
  3. Set a Monthly cap if the law limits it (leave blank for none) and save.
Personal relief. Without at least a personal relief, PAYE is charged on the full tax.

Rates & Limits

The remaining statutory figures are kept as settings, each with a Key payroll looks for, a Name and a Value. Filter by Category, and use the edit button to change a value.

KeyWhat it is
ShifRateSHIF, as a percentage of gross pay (2.75).
ShifMinimumThe smallest SHIF deduction a month.
ShifRoundingUnit1 rounds SHIF to the nearest shilling; 0 keeps the cents.
AhlRateThe Housing Levy taken from the employee, as a percentage of gross pay (1.5).
AhlEmployerRateThe school's matching Housing Levy, as a percentage of gross pay. It is a cost to the school, never deducted from pay.
RetirementReliefPercentNSSF and pension contributions come off taxable income up to this percentage of basic pay...

Keep the keys exactly. Payroll reads these by their key, and only while they are active. Change the value, never the key; a setting with a mistyped key, or an inactive one, counts as 0.

Approval and Access

  • Approval. Under Settings > Approval Workflows, the Payroll workflow sets who reviews and who approves a processed payroll. While it is active with at least one step, payroll officers submit each month for approval and only the final approver's approval locks it. If it is switched off, a payroll officer approves with a single click on the Processing page.
  • Payroll officers. Give the payroll officers' role the Payroll pages under Security > Menu Permissions. Anyone holding Processing can open every employee's payslips, including ones not yet approved; everyone else sees only their own, once approved.
  • My Payslips. A member of staff sees their own payslips only when their user account is linked to their staff record.

Elections Set-up

Elections need little set-up: three defaults in Global Settings, and the right people given the right Elections pages. Running an election is described in the user guide.

Elections Settings

Open Settings > Global Settings and click Elections Settings. Change a value, then click Save changes at the top.

SettingWhat it does
Voter registerThe register type a new election starts with. Frozen (strict): the register is a snapshot taken when nominations close, so a student who changes class afterwards votes where they were registered. Live (lenient): who may vote follows each student's current class until they vote. Frozen is the default.
Results screen refresh (seconds)How often a new election's live results screen refreshes. Default: 10.
Order of names on the ballotAlphabetical: every ballot lists the candidates in name order. Random: each ballot issued shows them in a fresh random order, so no candidate always appears first. Takes effect for the next ballot issued.

The first two are only defaults: the elections officer can change the register type and the refresh time on each election (the register type only until the register is taken). The ballot order applies to every election.

Who Does What

Access to elections follows the three Elections pages a role holds under Security > Menu Permissions.

PageGiven at installation toWhat it allows
Manage ElectionsAdministrator, HeadTeacher, DeputyHeadTeacherMakes the user an elections officer: create and edit elections, set positions, nominate and approve candidates, set up polling stations, move the stages, see the live count, draw lots, declare the results and record the winners. Elections officers can also run any polling station.
Polling StationAdministrator, HeadTeacher, DeputyHeadTeacher, Teacher, HeadOfDepartmentLets a returning officer open the stations they have been given and issue ballots there. Users without Manage Elections see only their own stations.
ResultsAdministrator, HeadTeacher, DeputyHeadTeacherOpens the results page. The live count before the results are declared is shown only to elections officers; once declared, the results are open to anyone with this page.

Users with the Administrator or SuperAdministrator role are always elections officers. The dashboard's Elections card is given to the roles that have Manage Elections.

Returning officers

A returning officer is chosen per polling station, not by role. Any user whose account is linked to a staff record can be picked. A station per class picks each class's class teacher (from the class leadership roles) automatically, when they have a login. So, before an election:

  • Make sure each class teacher has a user account linked to their staff record, and is set as the class teacher of their class.
  • Make sure the role they hold has the Polling Station page.
Suggested arrangement. Give Manage Elections only to the one or two staff who run the elections (for example the deputy head and the teacher in charge of student leadership), Polling Station to all teachers, and Results to the school's leadership. Student voters need no account: they vote on the returning officer's screen.
Adding a page to a role. A role with no menu permissions at all sees the whole menu. Giving it a single Elections page would hide everything else, so add the Elections pages only to roles that already have their pages set.

Communications Set-up

Before the school can send SMS and email, four things are set up: the SMS gateway and email account (a SuperAdministrator's job), the wording and channel of each standard message, where parents' contacts are taken from, and who may send. This chapter takes them in that order. Day-to-day sending, the queue and the SMS report are in the user guide's Communications chapter.

Gateway Settings

Communications > Gateway Settings holds the SMS provider account (TextSMS) and the email (SMTP) account. Only a SuperAdministrator can open it; anyone else sees "Only a SuperAdministrator can view or change the gateway settings."

The banner at the top says which state the school is in: Live (green), where messages go to their real recipients, or Test mode is on (yellow).

Setting up SMS

Get these details from your SMS provider's account first.

  1. API URL: leave the provider's address as it is unless they give you another. Left blank, ShuleNova uses the TextSMS default.
  2. API key: paste the key from the provider. Once saved it shows a green stored badge and is never shown again; leave the box blank to keep it, or paste a new one to replace it.
  3. Partner ID: from the provider.
  4. Sender ID: the name parents see as the sender, up to 11 characters. It must be the sender ID the provider registered for the school, spelled exactly the same.
  5. Price per SMS part (KES): what the provider charges per part. It is used only to value the monthly SMS report.
  6. Switch on Enabled in the blue bar and click Save settings.

SMS cannot be switched on without the API key, partner ID and sender ID; saving says which is missing, for example "The SMS sender ID is not set. SMS cannot be switched on without it." Check SMS balance asks the provider how many SMS credits remain.

Setting up email

  1. Fill in SMTP host, Port and SSL / TLS from your email provider. Port 465 uses SSL from the start; 587 upgrades with STARTTLS.
  2. Username and Password of the mailbox. Like the API key, the saved password shows stored; leave it blank to keep it.
  3. From email and From name (usually the school's name) are what parents see; Reply-to email is where their replies go, if not to the From address.
  4. Switch on Enabled in the email bar and click Save settings. Email cannot be switched on without the host, the port and the from email.

Test mode

Use test mode while you set up, train staff or check wording. With Test mode on:

  • no message reaches a real parent or staff member;
  • up to 3 messages of each send go to the Test phone number and Test email instead, so you can see how they arrive; the rest are only recorded;
  • with no test number or email, nothing leaves the system at all; messages are only marked as sent so the queue and wording can be checked;
  • test messages show a Test badge in the queue and are not counted in the SMS report.

The test phone number must be a valid Kenyan mobile number. Test messages to the test number go out even while SMS is switched off, which is how you try the gateway before going live.

Trying the gateways

Under Try the gateways, type a phone number or email and click Send test SMS or Send test email. This sends one real message straight away, whatever test mode says, so save the settings first. The result appears at once (for example "The gateway refused the SMS: ..."), and the message is recorded in the queue as a test.

Going live. 1. Enter the SMS (and email) details and save. 2. Send a test SMS to your own phone. 3. With test mode on, send a real notice to one class and read it on the test phone. 4. Switch test mode off and save. From then on every send reaches real parents.

Message Types and Templates

Communications > Templates sets the wording of everything the school sends. Only Administrators and SuperAdministrators can change it.

Standard message types

These are the messages sent by the "send to parents" buttons on the results and fee pages. They cannot be deleted.

Message typeSent from
Exam resultsReleasing an exam or the send button on School Exams, and the Exam Results report.
New fee invoiceStudent Invoices.
Fee balance reminderFee Reports, Outstanding and Balances by Class.
Fee payment receivedFee Payments, for a family's receipt. Switched off when the module is installed.
Fee payment by a sponsorFee Payments, for a sponsor's receipt: tells the parents the sponsor paid. Starts the same as Fee payment received.
  • Send by chooses SMS, Email or SMS & Email. The change is saved as soon as you pick it.
  • The Active switch turns the message type on or off, also saved at once. While it is off, its send buttons answer "The '...' message type is switched off. Turn it on under Communications > Templates."
  • The edit button opens the wording.

Editing the wording

  1. Click the edit button on the message type or template.
  2. Change the SMS text, and the Email subject and Email body when it is sent by email. The email body is plain text; line breaks are kept.
  3. To add a recipient's details, click in the text where they go, then click a placeholder on the right. Each message type has its own: exam results offer {SubjectScores}, {MeanGrade} and {Position}; invoices {InvoiceNumber}, {DueDate} and {Balance}; payments {AmountPaid} and {ReceiptNumber}; and so on.
  4. Click Save.

The count under the SMS text is before placeholders are filled in. Names, subject scores and amounts make the real message longer; the preview when sending shows the real number of parts. The exam results SMS often runs to two parts per parent. An active message type must have the text its channel needs: "Enter the SMS text, or send this message type by email only."

Saved templates for custom messages

These are ready wording for Send Message, such as a closing-day notice or a meeting reminder.

  1. Click New template.
  2. Give it a Name (required) and, if you like, a Description, choose Send by, and leave Active ticked.
  3. Type the text, using the placeholders on the right, and click Save.

Only active templates are offered on Send Message. The red bin deletes a template; messages already sent with it are kept.

Where Parents' Contacts Come From

Settings > Global Settings, Communications Settings section, has one setting, Parent Contact Source: where the phone number and email for messages to parents are taken from.

ChoiceContacts used
Parent record, else student recordThe linked parents' phone and email; for a learner whose linked parents have none, the contact on the learner's own record. This is the default.
Parent record onlyOnly the linked parents. Learners with no reachable parent are skipped.
Student record onlyOnly the contact on the learner's record. Suits a school whose records came from an older system, with the guardian's phone kept on the learner.

When a learner has several linked parents and some are marked Notifiable? on their parent record, only those get messages; otherwise all the learner's active parents do. Phone numbers must be Kenyan mobile numbers (07..., 01... or 254...); a learner or parent with none is listed as skipped in the preview. Keep parents' numbers up to date under Students > Parents: that is the surest way to cut failed messages.

Change the setting, then click Save changes at the top of the page.

Who Can Send

Three checks decide what a user can do:

  1. The module. Without Communications in the school's licence, the menu and every send button are hidden.
  2. Page access, from the role's menu permissions (Security > Menu Permissions). Each Communications page shows in the menu of a role given that page. The "send to parents" buttons work for a role with access to Communications or to the page the button is on: School Exams, Exam Results, Student Invoices, Fee Payments, Fee Reports or Statements.
  3. The role. Some actions need a particular role on top of page access.
ActionRoles
Open the queue, the templates and the SMS report; preview a messageAnyone with page access
Send a message, or send to parents from another page; retry or cancel messagesAdministrator, SuperAdministrator, HeadTeacher, Accounts
Change message types and templatesAdministrator, SuperAdministrator
Gateway Settings: view, change, test, check the balanceSuperAdministrator

A user who lacks the role gets "You are not allowed to send messages." A View-only account can preview but nothing it sends is queued.

Example. To let the bursar send invoices and reminders but not class notices, give their role access to the fee pages and not to Communications > Send Message. With the Accounts role, the send buttons on those pages work for them.
