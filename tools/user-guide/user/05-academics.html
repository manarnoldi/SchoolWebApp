Academics

The Academics menu is where teachers record how learners are doing: CBE assessment levels against each specific learning outcome, marks in the school's exams, and the learner-development records (co-curricular activities, responsibilities, community service and values) that appear on the report form. Its Reports pages turn all of this into broadsheets, report forms and performance analysis.

You find it under Academics in the menu, in four groups: Assessments, Exams, Learner Development and Reports.

Who can enter what. A teacher enters assessments and exam marks only for the subjects and classes they are allocated to under Staff > Subject Allocation. Administrators can enter for any teacher, class and subject. So can any role given Student Assessments (Admin) in Menu Permissions (a Director of Studies, for example). That menu item is a permission, not a page: clicking it opens Student Assessments.

Most pages here open already filled in: the current year and term, a first class and subject, with the list loaded. Change any filter and click the page's Load button to see something else. The filters are kept in the page's address, so a refresh or a bookmark brings back the same view.

Student Assessments

Student Assessments is where you grade learners against the curriculum's specific learning outcomes, one sub-strand at a time. Each grade is a CBE performance level such as EE (Exceeding Expectations), ME (Meeting), AE (Approaching) or BE (Below), with its points in brackets: EE (4). Your school chooses the scale (4-point, or 8-point with levels like EE1 and EE2) in its grading settings.

Entering assessments for a sub-strand

  1. Open Academics > Assessments > Student Assessments. The page opens on the current term and one of your classes and subjects, with the sub-strand you assessed last.
  2. Check the filters across the top: Curriculum, Year, Session (the term), Class, Subject, then Strand and Sub-Strand. A Theme filter appears for subjects whose design groups strands into themes.
  3. On the second row choose the Assessment Type (for example Formative Assessments) and the Teacher. A teacher sees only their own name here. The Date is optional.
  4. Click Load Assessments. The grid lists every learner in the class, with one column per specific outcome in the sub-strand.
  5. Pick a level in each cell. Leave a cell on - if you have not assessed that outcome yet.
  6. Click Save All Assessments at the bottom. Only new or changed cells are sent. Saved cells turn green.
  • The header shows how many learners are listed and how many grades are saved. Use the search box to find a learner by admission number or name.
  • The Average row at the bottom shows the class's typical level for each outcome.
  • The small red × beside a saved grade deletes that one grade. Delete All Saved removes every saved grade in the grid, after a confirmation.
Not allocated? If you pick a class and subject you do not teach, a yellow warning says "You are not allocated to this subject for the selected class" and the save button is disabled. Ask your administrator to allocate the subject to you under Staff > Subject Allocation.
Tip. Grade as you go: after each sub-strand is taught, load it and enter the levels. The Assessment Tracker counts a sub-strand as due a week after the last scheme-of-work week that teaches it.

Assessment Tracker

The Assessment Tracker shows how far each teacher has got with formative assessments this term. It has one row per teacher, learning area and class. The scheme of work says which sub-strands are due for assessment, and the grades entered show which are done. Teachers see their own rows. A Head of Department sees their department(s) and their own learning areas. Administrators see the whole school. The line under the filters tells you which of these you are seeing.

Following up

  1. Pick the Term, then narrow with Teacher, Department, Grade, Learning area or the search box.
  2. Click a status chip (for example Behind) to show only those rows. Click it again, or clear, to show all.
  3. Click a row to open it. On the left are its sub-strands, with each outcome's number of learners graded. On the right are the learners missing grades.
  4. Click Grades on a sub-strand to open its grade sheet in Student Assessments. Back to tracker there returns you to the same view.

By teacher switches to one line per teacher, with their subject-classes by status, furthest behind first. Click a teacher to see their rows. The two buttons on the right export the list to Excel (CSV) and print it.

School Exams

A school exam is one sitting for the whole school in a term, such as the Opening Exam, Mid-Term Exam, End-Term Exam or a weekly exam. It sets the type, the dates and the mark-entry deadline. Each class and subject that sits it is then added under Register Exams. Usually the Director of Studies or an administrator sets these up.

Creating a school exam

  1. Open Academics > Exams > School Exams and check the Curriculum and Year.
  2. Click New School Exam.
  3. Choose the Term (Session) and Exam Type, then enter the Start Date and End Date.
  4. Set a Mark Entry Deadline if you want one. It cannot be earlier than the end date. After it passes, teachers can no longer enter marks for this exam, though administrators can still correct them.
  5. Add an optional Description (for example "Weekly Exam 3 - Fri 11 Sep") and click Save.

Releasing results

A new school exam is a Draft. When marking is finished, click the green bullhorn button on its row (Release results) to release it. Released results appear on the dashboard summary. The release box has a tick box, Send the results to parents now, that sends each learner's results to their parents. You can also send them later with the paper-plane button. A Parents notified badge shows once results have been sent. The yellow undo button on a released exam reverts the release. That hides the results from the dashboard again, but does not recall messages already sent.

The pencil edits a school exam. The bin deletes it, but only once its registered class and subject exams have been removed.

Register Exams

Registering says which classes sit which subjects in a school exam, and out of how many marks. Marks can only be entered for registered exams.

Registering a school exam's papers

  1. Open Academics > Exams > Register Exams and click Register New Exam(s).
  2. Choose the Curriculum, Year, Session (Term) and Education Level (or All Education Levels).
  3. Choose the School Exam. Its dates and mark-entry deadline show underneath. If the list is empty, create the school exam first.
  4. Enter the Exam Mark (what each paper is out of, 100 by default) and an optional description.
  5. Tick the Subjects and Classes, or use Select All. The button shows how many papers will be created, for example Register Exams (24).
  6. Click it and confirm. A subject that is not taught at a class's education level is skipped, and the confirmation says how many were skipped.

On the list, filter by Class or School Exam, or search by subject, class or type. The pencil changes a paper's Exam Mark or description. Its dates come from the school exam, so change those under School Exams. Tick rows and click Delete Selected to remove several at once.

Results Entry

Results Entry is where you type exam marks, a class at a time, with a column for each subject.

  1. Open Academics > Exams > Results Entry. It opens on the current term and the latest exam, with one of your classes loaded.
  2. To change it, choose the Session, Class and School Exam, and click Load Grid.
  3. Type each learner's score. Each column heading shows the subject's short name and what it is out of, such as MAT (100). A score above that, or below zero, is cleared with a warning.
  4. Click Save All Results. Only new or changed scores are saved. Saved scores show green.
  • A teacher sees only the subjects they are allocated to in that class. A grey cell is a subject the learner does not take.
  • The red × beside a saved score deletes it. Delete All Results deletes every saved score in the grid.
  • Once the school exam's mark entry deadline has passed, a teacher sees "The mark entry deadline for this exam has passed. Contact the administrator for any corrections."
Two other entry pages. The same marks can be entered one subject at a time, with an optional remark per learner (CBE Exams: Exam Results, /cbe/exams/exam-results). They can also be entered one learner at a time across all their subjects (CBE Exams: Results (Classwise), /cbe/exams/exam-results-classwise). Neither has a menu item of its own. Open them by typing the address after your school's ShuleNova address. They save to the same results as Results Entry.

Learner Development

These pages record the parts of a learner's growth that are not exam marks. Each one prints in its own section of the report form. They all work the same way: the filters at the top, Load or Load Students, then a row per learner, and one save button at the bottom that sends only what changed. A search box finds a learner by admission number or name.

Co-curricular

Scores each learner in a co-curricular activity (music, football, scouts and so on) on the school's score types, such as Participation, Achievement and Effort.

  1. Open Academics > Learner Development > Co-curricular. Choose the Year, Class and Activity, and click Load.
  2. For each learner who takes part, pick a rating under each score type and add remarks if you wish. The Overall remark is printed on the report form for that activity.
  3. Click Save All Scores. A learner who is given a score is enrolled in the activity automatically.

The bin on a row removes that learner from the activity, with their scores.

Responsibilities

Records the responsibilities a learner holds (class duties, keeping the environment clean) and the social skills they show (sharing, teamwork), for the year.

  1. Open Academics > Learner Development > Responsibilities. Choose the Year and Class, and click Load Students.
  2. In the Add column pick a responsibility or social skill and click +. It appears in the middle column, marked R (responsibility) or S (social skill), with a box for remarks.
  3. Click Save All Assignments.

The red × removes an item. An item that was already saved is removed straight away, after you confirm.

Community Service

Works like Responsibilities, but for each term: choose the Year, Session and Class, and click Load Students. Add the community-service activities each learner took part in, with remarks, and click Save All Assignments.

Values

Rates each learner each term on the values your school has set up (love, responsibility, respect, unity, peace, patriotism and so on).

  1. Open Academics > Learner Development > Values. Choose the Year, Session and Class, and click Load Students.
  2. Under each value, pick a rating (for example Meets Expectation) and add an optional remark.
  3. Click Save All Scores.

Exam Results and Report Forms

Academics > Reports > Exam Results gathers the exam reports on one page, as tabs: Exam Results, Report Forms, Annual Report Forms, Missing Marks, Class Exam Progress, Student Exam Progress and Year Progress.

The broadsheet (Exam Results tab)

The broadsheet lists a class's marks in one exam, with each learner's total, mean and rank.

  1. Choose the Session, Class and School Exam, and click Load.
  2. Read the subjects across, then Total M (marks), Total P (points), Mean M, Mean P, Mean G (grade) and Rank. The badge above the table shows how learners are ranked, for example by mean marks. Your school sets this.
  3. Print it with MKS (marks), PTS (points), GRD (grades) or the combined broadsheet button.

The green Send button sends each learner's results to their parents.

Printing report forms

The report form is the term's report for each learner: exam results, the CBE sections (values, co-curricular, responsibilities, community service), and lines for the class teacher's and head teacher's comments, which are written by hand.

  1. Open the Report Forms tab. Choose the Year, Session and Class, and click Load.
  2. For one learner, click the eye button to preview their report, or the printer button to print it.
  3. For several, tick them (the box beside # ticks the whole class) and click Preview or Print. All the reports come out as one PDF, each learner starting on a new page.

The preview opens as a PDF in a new browser tab. A progress bar shows while a large class is being generated. Annual Report Forms works the same way but puts all of the year's terms on one form, so it asks only for the year and class.

Missing Marks

Before printing report forms, check the Missing Marks tab. Choose the year, curriculum, term and school exam and click Search. It lists every learner with a registered paper that has no mark, and the subject codes missing. Print gives the list to hand to subject teachers.

Exam progress

  • Class Exam Progress follows a class through every exam of one type in a term (the weekly exams, say), oldest first. Each mark shows the change from that learner's previous exam. Choose the term, class and Exam type, click Load Report, and pick a Subject or keep the average. Print prints the class list with its chart.
  • Student Exam Progress shows the same for one learner, subject by subject, with a trend for each. It prints for one learner, or for all of them, a page each in one PDF.
  • Year Progress shows every exam of the year for a learner, against the class mean, as on the learner's own page. Choose the year and class, click Load, and print one learner or all.

Assessment Report

The Assessment Report is the CBE progress report: for each learner, every subject's strands, sub-strands and specific outcomes, with the level they reached this term and the grading key.

  1. Open Academics > Reports > Assessment Report. Choose the Year, Session and Class, and click Load.
  2. Click the eye button to preview one learner's report, or the printer button to print it.
  3. To print several, tick them and click Print Selected. They come out as one PDF.

Subject Performance

Subject Performance ranks the subjects in each class for one exam. For each subject it shows the number of entries, the mean percentage, grade and points, the top learner(s) and the subject teacher. Choose the Session and School Exam, click Load, and Print if you need it on paper.

Subject Performance Trend

The second tab follows one subject in one class across every released exam of the year. It is useful for a subject teacher's or department's analysis.

  1. Choose the Year, Class and Subject, and click Load.
  2. Read the Performance Analysis (learners who sat, highest, lowest and mean score, and the mean grade for each exam), the Performance Distribution (how many learners at each level), the two charts, and the written Analysis that ShuleNova drafts from the figures.
  3. Choose the exam under Term / Exam, type your Conclusion & Action Plan for it, and click Save. The plan is kept for that subject, class and exam.
  4. Preview prints this subject's analysis. Print All Subjects builds one PDF with a page for every subject in the class, each with its action plan for the chosen exam.

Class Performance

Class Performance ranks the classes in one exam by their average. Classes are grouped, and ranked within each group, by education level (pre-primary, lower primary, and so on). Each row shows the number of learners, the class average and grade, the top learner and the class teacher. Choose the Session and School Exam and click Load.

The Class Performance Trend tab does for a whole class what Subject Performance Trend does for one subject: the class's results across the year's exams, charts, a written analysis, and an action plan you can save and print.

Student Subject Allocation

This report lists every learner in a class with the subjects they are allocated, so you can check that each learner takes the right subjects before exams are registered and marks entered. Choose the year, curriculum, level and class, and click Search. Click Print for a printed copy. Subjects are allocated under Students > Subject Allocation.
